How the model works

Range: every figure in the revenue table is computed live from the inputs — nothing is hard-coded. Each stream is computed twice, at the minimum assumptions and at the central assumptions, and shown as a band. Change any assumption — minimum or central — and its end of every band moves, including the lower end.

Occupancy rule: each token sells only its asset’s spare capacity. Radio spare falls as own traffic grows (subscribers × data per sub, measured against the site type’s reference load); compute spare falls as the GPU pool’s own use rises. With occupancy accounted for, token income rises only by raising the token’s base value — never by assuming spare that own traffic has already taken.

Slice shares re-weight the Stream 1 price (URLLC premium for latency, mIoT discount for bulk). Stream 2 is the thinner-evidence stream: GPU utilisation and VM pricing are reasonable assumptions, not measured data. Personal VMs complement handset AI (needed anyway for latency/privacy/offline) and are billed separately. All prices are assumed until a registry publishes cleared prices — the claim is the margin of clearance. The GPU pool is per site, so per-subscriber GPU income rises when subscribers per site fall — quote a per-subscriber figure only with its subscribers-per-site assumption. Rural GPU pool is 1 GPU only (24 GPU-hours/day, minimum and central). Direct GPU/VM access is open access, not tied to a cellular subscription: anyone who can reach the site’s edge server — over the site network or the internet, not necessarily through the cellular interface — can buy spare GPU/VM hours. It is therefore a separate revenue ingress for the carrier, apart from serving its own subscribers’ handset-app inference. Open access still sits under RAN pre-emptive priority, and the token states performance class and window.

India — Stream 1 anchor: a 3.3 GHz auction carried a reserve price of about Rs 40 crore/MHz; amortised over the licence area and the sellable window (about 1,500 hr/yr of the 20-year term) that is about Rs 9/MHz·hr·km². The adopted base prices — urban Rs 5, suburban Rs 2.5, rural Rs 1 — sit at or below that anchor and scale down with circle prices. Data per sub defaults to 25.51 GB/month as the reference load. Occupancy is measured against each country’s own default data per sub.

India — GPU/VM use: AI app use in this country is far larger in absolute terms than in the other example, so the GPU pool is set higher here: 48 GPU-hr/day, VM utilisation 60–70%, against 24 GPU-hr/day, 40–60% in the other example — as stated assumptions, not measured site data.

South Africa — Stream 1 price: ICASA’s March 2022 IMT auction raised R14.48 billion for 306 MHz (about R47 million/MHz); over 62.0 million people (Census 2022) that is about R0.76 per MHz-pop; × 2,500 people/km² ÷ (20-year licence term × 1,500 hr/yr) ≈ R0.06/MHz·hr·km². Blended prices R0.07 / R0.11 at 2,500/km² reference density, scaled by each site’s density . Licence term: ICASA ITA, GN 717 of 2021, GG 45628, s8 (20 years). Urban preset density is now about 3,000/km², rounded from the City of Johannesburg municipal average of 2,924/km² (Stats SA Census 2022, Table 2.3, p. 17), stated as the site density assumption; national average about 52/km². Suburban 800/km², rural 50/km² (assumed). Low band: SA 700/800 MHz lots are 2×5 or 2×10 MHz (ICASA ITA), so the low-band option uses at most a 10 MHz carrier. A radio token is not offered where its gross is below the seller cost. A spectrum-weighted 26%/74% low/mid blend would give R0.082/R0.128 at the reference density instead of R0.07/R0.11; the paper (§6.1) reports that alternative without adopting it, so the headline totals are unchanged.

South Africa — GPU/VM: wholesale GPU-hour R92 = R67,000/month for a full NVIDIA RTX Pro 6000 server in Johannesburg (HOSTAFRICA, reported by TechCentral/MyBroadband, 19 March 2026, promoted content) ÷ 730 hr; South African monthly list prices give about R48–R103 per GPU-hour (HOSTAFRICA RTX Pro 6000 R35,000/month; CloudAfrica H200 R75,000/month excl. VAT; both accessed 3 Nov 2026). VM-hour R23–R30 assumed. Data per sub defaults to 6.3 GB/month: Vodacom South Africa average usage per smart device, year ended 31 March 2026 (Vodacom Integrated Report 2026, p. 49). Cross-check: MTN South Africa, year ended 31 December 2025, 26.5 GB per active postpaid data subscriber and 4.2 GB per prepaid customer (MTN Group FY2025 results, 16 March 2026). AI app use in absolute terms is far smaller here than in the other example, so the GPU pool is set lower: 24 GPU-hr/day, VM utilisation 40–60% — as stated assumptions, not measured site data. Token costs are a separate input to VM margin, not income: illustrative 20 queries/day × 2,000 tokens, 40% output = about 1.2M tokens/month, about $0.42/month for a low-cost model class vs about $18/month for a frontier class, plus delivery, billing, care, 15% VAT and currency exposure — all labelled assumptions, not measured.
